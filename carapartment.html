Option A: Buying a Car

Buying with cash is the cheapest option overall — no interest, no fees, no leasing premiums. You become full owner and face no mileage restrictions or return obligations. Financing (Autokredit) lets you spread the cost while building ownership.

Advantages of Buying
  • You own the car — it's an asset you can sell anytime
  • No mileage limits, no wear-and-tear penalties
  • Act as "Barzahler" at dealer → negotiate 5–15% discount
  • Free insurance choice (no mandatory Vollkasko)
  • Can pay off early after 6 months, no penalty
Disadvantages of Buying
  • Large upfront cost or long-term debt
  • You bear full depreciation risk
  • Repair costs increase as the car ages
  • Bank may hold car title as collateral until payoff
💡 Pro Tip

Buying a quality "Jahreswagen" (1-year-old car, often from dealer/manufacturer employees) avoids the steepest depreciation while still getting a near-new vehicle with warranty.

Option B: Leasing a Car

Car leasing means paying a monthly rate for a fixed period (typically 24–48 months), then returning the vehicle. You never own the car.

Two Main Types of Private Leasing

Kilometerleasing

Mileage-based — Rate calculated on expected annual km (e.g., 10,000–15,000 km/year).

Excess mileage costs €0.05–€0.15 per km. Safer option for private individuals.

Restwertleasing

Residual value-based — Rate based on car's projected end-of-lease value.

If car is worth less than projected, you pay the difference. Carries more risk.

Advantages of Leasing
  • Lower monthly payments than financing a purchase
  • Always drive a new car with latest technology
  • No resale hassle — just return it
  • No depreciation risk (with Kilometerleasing)
  • Lower or no down payment possible
  • Great for people unsure about long-term plans
Disadvantages of Leasing
  • Never build ownership — money is "gone" at the end
  • Mileage restrictions — exceeding them is costly
  • Return condition scrutiny can trigger charges
  • Early termination is very expensive or impossible
  • Repeatedly leasing is more expensive than buying long-term
⚡ EV Subsidy 2026

Germany reintroduced EV incentives in 2026 with a €3 billion program. Subsidies of up to €6,000 for both purchases and leases of BEVs (income-dependent, must keep vehicle 36+ months). Applications expected to open May 2026, retroactive from Jan 1, 2026.

📊 Total Monthly Cost Example

A 70 m² apartment in a mid-range area: Kaltmiete ~€1,400 + Nebenkosten ~€250 + Electricity ~€70 + Internet ~€35 = ~€1,750 all-in

Advantages of Renting
  • Full flexibility to relocate
  • No large capital commitment
  • Landlord handles major maintenance
  • Strong tenant protection laws (Mieterschutz)
  • No transaction costs (no taxes, notary, etc.)
  • No property value risk
Disadvantages of Renting
  • Rent is a pure expense — no equity building
  • Rents keep rising (3–5% annually)
  • Extremely competitive market
  • Limited ability to modify the apartment
  • New tenants pay ~50% more than long-standing ones
  • Many apartments unfurnished (buy your own kitchen!)

When to Buy vs. When to Rent

🏠 Buying Makes Sense
  • You plan to stay 10+ years
  • You have 20–30% equity available
  • Stable income for monthly payments + reserves
  • Want to build long-term wealth through property
  • Prices stabilized → possible entry window before resuming growth
🔑 Renting Makes Sense
  • Uncertain about long-term plans in Munich
  • Don't have significant savings for down payment + closing costs
  • Value flexibility (job changes, relocating)
  • Income-to-price ratio doesn't support comfortable payments
  • Prefer investing capital elsewhere at potentially higher returns
